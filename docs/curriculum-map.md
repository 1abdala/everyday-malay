# Everyday Malay curriculum map

**Status:** proposed curriculum architecture. Only the three rows explicitly marked **existing draft** have playable content. Every other chapter is a planned outline, not a promised or reviewed lesson. This map is not a proficiency certification framework.

## Design rules

- Teach useful exchanges from beginner prerequisites toward longer and less predictable communication.
- Start with comprehension and stable phrases; then ask learners to retrieve and adapt those phrases in a real-world goal.
- Recycle earlier language in later situations rather than treating each scene as an isolated card.
- Keep regional/register claims attached to reviewed contexts. The current scenarios target colloquial Klang Valley / West Coast Malay where specified; expansion needs reviewer decisions per script.
- Separate lexical coverage from communication outcome. A learner may complete a scenario with a hint; that is not evidence of independent mastery.

## Proposed path overview

| Level | Purpose | Expected ability at level end |
|---|---|---|
| 1. First Conversations | Basic greetings, simple questions, polite responses, and familiar purchases. | Handle a short, predictable exchange with a few practiced phrases and ask for simple clarification. |
| 2. Everyday Errands | Food, transport, directions, prices, and everyday requests. | Complete familiar errands, understand common replies, and adapt a few reusable patterns. |
| 3. Markets & Local Life | Night markets, services, appointments, and local routines with more variation. | Ask follow-up questions, compare options, confirm details, and repair minor misunderstandings. |
| 4. Handle the Unexpected | Problems, changes, explanations, and less predictable responses. | Explain needs, negotiate alternatives, and recover when a rehearsed phrase is not enough. |
| 5. Speak More Naturally | Longer exchanges, conversational signals, register choice, and varied speakers. | Sustain a practical interaction across turns and adapt politely to context; no “native-like” claim. |

## Common chapter template

1. Real-life goal and two or three observable outcomes.
2. A small set of essential phrases, with context and audio status disclosed.
3. Reusable sentence pattern(s) with one or two meaningful substitutions.
4. Short dialogue with listen-first and optional translation.
5. One comprehension check about speaker intent or next action.
6. Guided retrieval with a hint ladder.
7. Role-play with valid, reviewed alternatives and recoverable errors.
8. Result scoped to this task, including what was independently retrieved versus supported.
9. Brief lesson summary and next useful action.
10. Later spaced review of a few missed/difficult items, always available without XP gates.

Not all chapters need all exercise types. Add an activity only when it tests a stated outcome.

## Prerequisite graph and implementation guidance

`Greetings/introductions → polite basics/repair → Shop → Mamak → Grab → price/directions → markets/services → unexpected changes → integrated independent tasks`.

The current prototype compresses three applied scenarios into Starter Chapter 01 and makes Explore open. Keep that behavior until there is actual reviewed content for the prerequisite chapters; do not render invented playable placeholders or claim learners have completed a larger curriculum.
